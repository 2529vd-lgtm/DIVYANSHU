Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)

Polity · Part III of the Constitution

Fundamental Rights are in Part III of the Indian Constitution and are justiciable: you can go to court if they are violated. Part III is called the Magna Carta of India.

Six categories

  1. Right to Equality (Art. 14–18): equality before law, no discrimination, equal opportunity in public employment, abolition of untouchability (17) and of titles (18).
  2. Right to Freedom (Art. 19–22): six freedoms under Art. 19, protection in respect of conviction (20), life and personal liberty (21), right to education (21A), protection against arrest and detention (22).
  3. Right against Exploitation (Art. 23–24): bans human trafficking and forced labour, and child labour under 14 in hazardous work.
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Art. 25–28)
  5. Cultural & Educational Rights (Art. 29–30): protects minorities' culture and their right to run educational institutions.
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32): Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called it the "heart and soul" of the Constitution.

Key facts

  • Art. 12 defines "State"; Art. 13 makes laws that violate Fundamental Rights void.
  • The Right to Property (Art. 31) was removed by the 44th Amendment, 1978. It is now a legal right under Art. 300A.
  • Art. 21A (free and compulsory education for ages 6–14) was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002.
  • Art. 20 and 21 cannot be suspended, even during a national emergency.
  • There are five writs: Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari and Quo Warranto.

Important cases

  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): the Basic Structure doctrine.
  • Maneka Gandhi (1978): widened Art. 21 to require "procedure established by law" to be just, fair and reasonable.
  • K.S. Puttaswamy (2017): Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right.
Exam tip: remember the article ranges in order (14, 19, 23, 25, 29, 32), since many questions are direct article-number questions.
